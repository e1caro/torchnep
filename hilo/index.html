A shoe that has lost its small cards is rich in tens and aces, and that favours the player. Hi-Lo tracks this with one number. Every card you see moves the count by its tag:

Counting well is five small skills running at once. Each stage below has a drill and a standard to meet before you move on. Your results are kept on this device.

    What the edge is worth

    These figures come from a 60-million-hand simulation of the standard rules (six decks, the dealer stands on soft 17, double after split, bets of 1 to 12 units), one player against the dealer, 75% of the shoe dealt, no mistakes. Under Rules and bankroll you can change the rules, and The edge then runs the simulation for yours.

    • The edge is small and the swings are large. At 100 hands an hour you expect about 1.2 units an hour, with a typical hourly swing of about 31 units either way. After 200 hours, roughly 29% of perfect counters are still behind.
    • Bankroll decides whether you survive the swings. Betting this ramp without ever resizing, the chance of losing the whole bankroll is about 29% with 500 units, 8% with 1,000 and under 1% with 2,000.
    • Mistakes cost more than the edge. A basic strategy error rate of a few percent, or a count that drifts by two or three, gives it all back. That is why the standards below are strict.
    • Game choice matters as much as skill. Avoid tables that pay 6 to 5 on blackjack, continuous shuffling machines, and shoes cut off before 70% is dealt. None of them can be beaten by this method.
    • Counting in your head is legal. Devices are not. Casinos are private businesses and in most places can ask a suspected counter to stop playing blackjack, restrict them to flat bets, shuffle early, or bar them. A 1 to 12 bet spread is what they look for.

    Running count

    Start at 0. Add 1 for 2 to 6, subtract 1 for tens and aces, ignore 7 to 9. Read cards in pairs when you can: a king with a 5 is zero, so skip it. One card is held back from the deck, so a full countdown ends on +1, 0 or −1.

    Not passed Standard: 51 cards in 30 seconds or less, right, three times running

    True count and bets

    A running count of +6 means much more with one deck left than with five. Divide the running count by the decks still in the shoe and round down (−1.5 becomes −2). That true count sets the bet.

    The bet ramp

    Each point of true count is worth about half a percent. The house edge is gone at about +1, so the money goes out from +2. One unit is your table minimum.

    Deviations
